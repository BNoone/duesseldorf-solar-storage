About this page

What this models

A thought experiment: every suitable roof in Duesseldorf carries solar, and what heat and cooling demand do to that. It is not a forecast, and it is not a plan anyone has proposed.

What counts as a suitable roof

A building qualifies once its roof faces sum to at least 10 kW. North-facing pitched faces are excluded. Flat roofs count, since panels on them are angled south regardless of the roof's raw tilt.

Where the numbers come from

Roof potential: Solarkataster NRW. What is already installed: the Marktstammdatenregister, Germany's renewable energy registry. Weather, for the heatwave comparison: ERA5, via Open-Meteo. The city's own electricity use: Duesseldorf's Energie- und Treibhausgasbilanz 2022. Duesseldorf's household count: the city's own Amt für Statistik und Wahlen.

The cooling balance

Only 6% of German households own air conditioning today (Umweltbundesamt), so the AC ownership control asks what happens as that share rises, at 50% and 90%. The cooling figure is computed bottom-up: Duesseldorf's own household count × AC ownership share × 3 kW per single-split unit × a 0.5 diversity factor (not every unit runs at once), method and both figures from Jan Rosenow, drawing on Andreou et al. 2020. This is power at one hour, the heatwave afternoon's own peak, never converted to energy over a day and never used to infer an hourly demand curve.

Supply above is modelled hour by hour from real ERA5 weather. Demand is not: there is no hourly electricity-consumption dataset for Duesseldorf, so the cooling balance is a single computed figure at one hour, not a modelled curve.
